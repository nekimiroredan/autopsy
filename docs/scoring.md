# Scoring

A candidate survives only if replay closes every gap it claims to explain within (10^{-6}) units. The chance likelihood ratio is the number of independent search opportunities divided by the number that close the gap. Duplicate references multiply the ratio by 3, a nearby numeric document-sequence gap multiplies it by 2, and missing references on matched transactions multiply it by 0.8 at Tier 1. A cutoff edit that also closes the next anchored gap gets a factor of 2. The likelihood ratio is capped at 50. Recount and residual-loss repairs use a likelihood ratio of 1 because they close the gap by construction.

The base prior is a fixed distribution over the nine repair causes. Feedback blends label proportions into the prior from global to event type, location, worker, and shift. A slice with (n) labels receives weight (min(n/(n+10), 0.75)). Worker and shift require Tier 1. The latest label for a discrepancy supplies its active vote. “Not sure” supplies no vote.

Rank by prior times likelihood ratio. Normalize against the best candidate for each cause, retaining alternative edits within the leading cause for global assignment. The optimizer allows one repair per gap and prevents reuse of a transaction or count in another repair. Its objective adds the log probability relative to a 0.05 abstention baseline. A plain sum of log probabilities makes the empty assignment optimal because every term is nonpositive. The engine replays selected edits together and flags count changes that affect a later window.

Calibration maps top raw probability to observed top-cause correctness on validation data. Confidence for alternatives shares the remaining probability mass in the same proportions as the original ranking. The decision requires both the calibrated threshold and a minimum raw top-two margin. Recount, loss booking, and missing entries without corroborating document evidence remain tentative. An unanchored count has no expected balance and always abstains.

Hotspot exposure is the number of transactions in a slice. The numerator counts distinct transactions linked to selected or confirmed repairs. These observed rates depend on what the engine can verify and what reviewers confirm. The 95% credible intervals use a Jeffreys beta prior. A one-sided hypergeometric enrichment test compares each sufficiently exposed slice against its family, and the engine applies Benjamini–Hochberg across all tested slices. The counterfactual assumes the slice can reach its family median and values the excess using the mean available value at risk for its error transactions.
